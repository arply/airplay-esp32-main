Step 1

Measure

arply measures the room’s background noise, then raises its volume (the one AirPlay uses) from the lowest step until the test noise is clearly above it. The bar at the right of the chart shows the microphone.

arply plays the sweeps itself; the filters are calculated after every measurement. Each run plays three sweeps per channel (in stereo: left, right, left, …) and averages them. Measure 3–5 positions around the listening spot, phone at ear height, microphone (bottom edge) towards the speaker.

Optional

Bass

For small speakers. Lifts the real bass below the speaker’s limit, held back automatically near full scale, a little more at low volume. Only below a third of the limit is cut (there the driver just moves). Harmonics are optional: they add overtones of the deep bass above the limit, from which your ear fills in the note – start at 0 and raise them by ear.

Subwoofer

Crossover

This arply is its room’s subwoofer (set on the settings page). It plays the mono bass below the crossover; while it plays, the speakers in the same room get the matching high-pass and their bass block pauses (Linkwitz-Riley, 24 dB/octave).

Delay the subwoofer when it stands nearer to you than the speakers (1 ms ≈ 34 cm). Then try the polarity with music, AirPlay to the speakers and the subwoofer together: the setting with the fuller bass around the crossover is right.

Step 2

Filters

Advanced: target, range and manual filters

Smoothing follows frequency: “Normal” uses 1/12 octave in the bass up to 150 Hz, getting coarser up to 1/3 octave from 3 kHz. “Fine” halves the windows, “Coarse” doubles them.

Cuts only: room-mode peaks are trimmed, dips are not filled. The iPhone microphone’s roll-off below about 80 Hz looks like a dip and is therefore ignored.

Step 3

Listen

arply plays the sample with the current correction. Every change is heard live (not saved until you press “Save to arply”). For your own music, play it to arply via AirPlay – the correction applies there too.

Step 4

Save to arply

One shared preamp for all channels, so the balance stays intact. Saved filters survive a restart.
