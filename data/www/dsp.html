Step 1

Measure

Hold the phone at ear height where you listen, microphone (bottom edge) towards the speaker, and stay quiet. arply plays test sweeps; the correction follows every measurement. Measure 3–5 positions around your seat.

Options

Before the first measurement arply sets its level by itself: it listens to the room’s background noise, then raises its volume (the one AirPlay uses) from the lowest step until the test noise is clearly above it. The bar at the right of the chart shows the microphone. Each measurement plays three sweeps per channel (in stereo: left, right, left, …) and averages them.

Step 2

Correction

Filters
Advanced

Room modes – narrow peaks up to 500 Hz – are only cut: filling dips does not work in a room. The speaker’s balance is corrected broadly, both ways, from the response smoothed over an octave in the bass and half an octave above 500 Hz – but never boosted below the speaker’s bass limit: there bass enhancement takes over. Both share the filters; each stops once the rest is within about 1 dB.

The target lifts the bass below about 120 Hz and rolls the treble off gently above 1 kHz (per octave), as a well-balanced speaker measures in a room. The iPhone microphone rolls off below about 85 Hz, so a dip there is never filled. Smoothing (room modes) uses 1/12 octave in the bass up to 150 Hz, getting coarser up to 1/3 octave from 3 kHz; “Fine” halves the windows, “Coarse” doubles them.

Step 3 · optional

Hear the difference

For your own music, play it to arply via AirPlay – the correction applies there too.

Optional · small speakers

Bass enhancement

Lifts the real bass below the speaker’s limit, held back automatically near full scale, a little more at low volume. Only below a third of the limit is cut (there the driver just moves). Harmonics are optional: they add overtones of the deep bass above the limit, from which your ear fills in the note – start at 0 and raise them by ear.

Subwoofer

Crossover

This arply is its room’s subwoofer (set on the settings page). It plays the mono bass below the crossover; while it plays, the speakers in the same room get the matching high-pass and their bass block pauses (Linkwitz-Riley, 24 dB/octave).

“Measure” in step 1 sets level, delay and polarity: each speaker of this room plays a sweep, then the subwoofer. A new crossover re-aligns from the measurements. Or set them by ear (1 ms ≈ 34 cm).
